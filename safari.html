Safari sightings

Every animal I saw in Botswana and Zambia, June 2025

After three days on safari, I had exhausted my interest in looking at animals, and developed a new passion for making lists of animals. I now understand the appeal of birdspotting.

Highlights

The Bestiary

Mammals (26)

  • African scrub hare
  • African wild dog We saw a pack of five (including one heavily pregnant female) chase down a warthog and eat alive. Gruesome stuff.
  • Baboon They always walk single file. They sleep in the trees, where they are safe from most predators (except leopards!)
  • Banded mongoose
  • Black-backed jackal
  • Blue wildebeest Oh, look, sire, the herd is on the move.
  • Buffalo Buffalo do not feint charging like elephants do. If they are charging, they are CHARGING.
  • Bush buck The males’ horns can grow up to half a metre long!
  • Common warthog Extremely relaxed around people, they barely seem notice we’re there.
  • Elephant Elephants remember where other elephants have been killed. They cross the river to Namibia during the day to eat, and come back at night to sleep in Botswana (where it's safe from poachers).
  • Giraffe Very nervous about bending down to drink, and not just because it looks ridiculous - they are very vulnerable to predators when leaning over. Can kill a lion with one kick (if it lands right).
  • Hippopotamus Closest living relative is whales. Can hold breath for up to six minutes.
  • Impala Skittish and leggy. Inner monologue goes something like: “Grass. Grass. Grass. Lion!! … Grass.”
  • Kudu Females have smaller ears and eyes, and must travel in groups for safety. Males have enormous ears.
  • Leopard To find a leopard: look for a tree with spots.
  • Lion Usually kill by going for the throat. Defined hierarchy for eating: adult males eat first, then lionesses, then juvenile lions, then cubs.
  • Malbrouck
  • Red lechwe (water antelope)
  • Sable antelope
  • Slender mongoose
  • Spotted hyena Funny-looking fellow.
  • Springbok
  • Vervet monkey
  • Water buck Yassified donkey.
  • White rhino Zambia’s eleven white rhinos are protected by armed guards, 24/7.
  • Zebra The reason we can rides horses and not zebras isn't just about temperament – zebras have much weaker spines.
